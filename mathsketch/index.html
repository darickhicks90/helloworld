How to use it

Equations

  • Write neatly, one equation per line, leaving a gap between lines.
  • Draw a loop around what you want: a letter solves for it, a piece like 3 + 4 works it out, a whole equation solves it.
  • Give known values on their own lines (R = 4). Several equations together are solved as a system.
  • Exponents: write them small and raised. Fractions: a line with numbers above and below. Square root: draw √ with a roof over the inside.
  • For multiply, write numbers and letters next to each other (2x) or use a raised dot.

Shapes

  • Shape: draw the outline. Shaky lines are fine. If it guesses wrong, pick the right shape from the list.
  • Known: draw a bracket alongside a side (or a small arc inside a corner), then write the number next to it. Writing ? marks what you want.
  • Find: circle a side, a corner, or the whole shape (for area, perimeter, volume or surface area).
  • You can also tap any label on the drawing to type a value.

Not enough information?

SketchSolve checks whether your values pin the answer down. If they don't, it says so and lists which extra measurement would do it. If your values contradict each other, it tells you that too.
